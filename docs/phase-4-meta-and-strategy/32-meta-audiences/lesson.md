# Lesson 32: Meta audiences

**Phase 4 · Week 7 · Day 2** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

On Meta, you don't target searches. You target people. Meta's audience tools range from precise,
such as "people who added to cart in the last 7 days", to fully automated. Knowing when to guide
Meta and when to let it explore is a core paid social skill.

## Key ideas

### Three kinds of audiences

| Type | Built from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Saved audiences**, also called core | Location, age, gender, and detailed targeting such as interests and behaviors | Women 25–45 in the US interested in specialty coffee |
| **Custom audiences** | Your own data | Website visitors in the last 30 days |
| **Lookalike audiences** | People similar to a custom audience | The 1% of US users most similar to your customers |

### Custom audience sources

| Source | Example |
|---|---|
| **Website** (needs the Pixel or Conversions API) | All visitors 30 days, product viewers 14 days, add to cart 7 days, purchasers 180 days |
| **Customer list** | Uploaded emails or phone numbers. Meta hashes them for matching. |
| **Engagement** | People who engaged with your Instagram account or Facebook Page |
| **Video** | People who watched at least 50% of a video |
| **Lead form** | People who opened or submitted an instant form |

### Lookalike audiences

A lookalike finds people who resemble a **source** audience:

- The source must contain at least 100 people from one country. Larger, higher-quality sources
  work better: purchasers beat all visitors.
- The **size** runs from 1% to 10% of a country's population. 1% is most similar. 10% is broadest.

### Advantage+ audience and broad targeting

With **Advantage+ audience**, Meta finds the audience itself. Any audience details you add become
**suggestions**, not limits. Many advertisers now run **broad** targeting: just location and
perhaps age, and let the creative and conversion data guide delivery.

Why broad often works: Meta sees far more signals than you can choose from a menu. When it has
good conversion data, narrow manual targeting often just makes ads more expensive.

The saying in the industry: **the creative is the targeting.** A video about home espresso
attracts home espresso fans, whatever the targeting settings.

### Exclusions

Exclude people who shouldn't see an ad:

- Purchasers from prospecting ads aimed at new customers
- Recent converters from retargeting
- Employees, using a customer list

### Audience size

Too small an audience makes ads expensive and stops ad sets from exiting the learning phase.
Retargeting audiences for small sites can be tiny. Combine segments, such as all visitors plus
engagers, until the audience is big enough to deliver.

### Special ad categories

Ads about **housing, employment, financial products and services**, and **social issues,
elections, or politics** must be declared. Meta then restricts targeting, such as age, gender,
and postcode, to prevent discrimination.

## Common mistakes

- **Stacking many narrow interests:** Small, expensive audiences that limit learning.
- **No exclusions:** Prospecting budget spent on existing customers.
- **Lookalikes from low-quality sources:** All page likers, rather than buyers.
