# Lesson 38: Media planning

**Phase 4 · Week 8 · Day 3** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

Sooner or later, someone asks: "We have $10,000 next month. Where should it go, and what will we
get?" A media plan answers that with numbers and reasons. It's one of the most common tasks in
interviews and in the job, and it pulls together everything in this course.

## Key ideas

### What a media plan contains

1. **Objective:** The business goal and the KPI.
2. **Audience and funnel:** Who you're reaching at each stage.
3. **Channel mix:** Which channels, campaign types, and how much budget each gets.
4. **Forecast:** Expected results for each channel.
5. **Test plan:** What you'll try, and how you'll judge it.
6. **Measurement:** How you'll track and report.
7. **Risks:** What could go wrong, and the early warning signs.

The [media plan template](../../../templates/media-plan.md) follows this structure.

### Three ways to set a budget

| Method | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Top-down** | A share of revenue, such as 10% | Established businesses with a set marketing budget |
| **Bottom-up** | Goal → conversions needed → expected CPA → budget | When there's a clear target |
| **Test and learn** | 70% proven, 20% promising, 10% experimental | Splitting a budget once you have results to build on |

Bottom-up example: 400 sales needed × $20 expected CPA = $8,000 budget.

### Forecasting with funnel maths

Work through the funnel for each channel:

```
Search:  budget ÷ CPC = clicks          → clicks × CVR = conversions
Social:  budget ÷ CPM × 1,000 = impressions → impressions × CTR = clicks → clicks × CVR = conversions
```

Use your own historical numbers wherever you have them. Where you don't, use cautious estimates,
and say so. Good plans show a range: conservative, expected, and optimistic.

### Constraints that limit spend

- **Search demand:** You can't buy more brand searches than people make. Brand search budgets
  are capped by search volume.
- **Audience size:** Retargeting audiences only absorb so much before frequency climbs.
- **Diminishing returns:** Each extra dollar buys a little less than the one before. If CPA is
  $20 at $5,000, it won't stay $20 at $50,000. The cost of the **extra** conversions (marginal
  CPA) rises faster than the average.

### MER as the plan's scorecard

Because platforms overlap in what they claim, judge the whole plan on **MER**: total revenue ÷
total ad spend, from the business's own sales data. Set the target from break-even ROAS.

### Seasonality and pacing

- Demand changes through the year: holidays, payday, weather. Shift budget toward strong
  periods.
- **Pacing** means spending evenly toward the monthly budget. Check it weekly, so you don't run
  out by day 20 or underspend by day 30.

## Common mistakes

- **Forecasts with no stated assumptions:** Nobody can check or challenge them.
- **Assuming CPA stays flat as budget grows:** Diminishing returns always apply.
- **Putting everything in the channel with the best last-click ROAS:** It's usually the one that
  depends on the others.
