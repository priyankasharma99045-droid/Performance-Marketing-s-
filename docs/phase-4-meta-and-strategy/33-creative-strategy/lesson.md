# Lesson 33: Creative strategy

**Phase 4 · Week 7 · Day 3** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

As Meta automates targeting, placements, and bidding, the creative is the biggest lever you
still control. It decides who stops scrolling, who clicks, and, with broad targeting, who Meta
shows the ad to at all. Strong performance marketers spend more time on creative than on
settings.

## Key ideas

### Formats and sizes

| Format | Best for | Aspect ratio |
|---|---|---|
| **Single image** | One clear message or offer | 4:5 or 1:1 for feeds |
| **Video** | Demonstrating, storytelling | 9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 for feeds |
| **Carousel** | Several products, or steps in a story | 1:1 or 4:5 |
| **Collection** | Browsing products from one ad | Uses a catalog |

Supply a vertical 9:16 version of every ad. Reels and Stories are full-screen, and a square ad
looks out of place there. Keep text and faces away from the top and bottom of vertical videos,
where the app's buttons and captions sit.

### The anatomy of an ad

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Hook** (first 1–3 seconds, or the first line) | Stop the scroll |
| **Body** | Build desire: benefit, proof, demonstration |
| **Call to action** | Tell people exactly what to do next |
| **Primary text** | The copy above the ad. About 125 characters show before "See more". |
| **Headline** | The bold line below the image or video |
| **CTA button** | Shop now, Learn more, Sign up |

### Design for sound off

Many people watch with the sound off. Add captions and on-screen text, and make the first frame
understandable without audio.

### Creative angles

An **angle** is the core idea behind an ad. Test different angles, not tiny variations:

| Angle | Example for Pebble |
|---|---|
| **Problem and solution** | "Stale supermarket beans?" then fresh delivery |
| **Testimonial or user-generated content (UGC)** | A real customer filming their morning coffee |
| **Demonstration** | Unboxing and brewing in 15 seconds |
| **Comparison** | Cafe coffee at $5 compared with Pebble at $1.20 a cup |
| **Social proof** | "Join 2,000 home baristas" |
| **Founder story** | The roaster explaining why freshness matters |
| **Offer-led** | "20% off your first bag" |

### Native beats polished

Ads that look like normal posts, filmed on a phone with a real person talking, often outperform
studio productions. They feel like content, not advertising. Polished ads still have a place,
but test both.

### Creative diversity

Meta's delivery system works best with **genuinely different** creatives: different angles,
formats, and people. Ten versions of the same image with different background colors give it
little to work with. Three distinct concepts give it a lot.

### Using AI for creative

AI tools can speed up creative work:

- **Ideas:** Ask a chatbot for 20 hooks for a persona, then pick and rewrite the best 5.
- **Variations:** Meta's Ads Manager can generate text variations, background changes, and
  resized images.
- **Scripts:** Draft a video script outline, then make it sound human.

Rules for using AI:

1. **Check every claim.** AI invents facts, reviews, and numbers.
2. **Never fabricate testimonials or reviews.** It's dishonest, and it breaks advertising rules.
3. **Edit for your brand voice.** Unedited AI copy sounds generic.
4. **Review automated variations** before they run. Turn off features that produce
   off-brand results.

## Common mistakes

- **Testing button colors instead of angles:** Small changes rarely matter on social.
- **Only horizontal video:** Wastes most of the screen in Reels and Stories.
- **Burying the hook:** A slow logo intro loses most viewers in the first second.
