# Practice 25: Choose bid strategies and set budgets

**30 minutes** · **You need:** A calculator · **Save to:** `my-work/notes/lesson-25.md`

## Goal

Pick the right bid strategy for different situations, and calculate budgets that can hit a
goal.

## Steps

### Part 1: Four scenarios (15 minutes)

For each scenario, choose a bid strategy, any target, and write one sentence on why.

1. **A new account.** A yoga studio launches its first Search campaign. Conversion tracking was
   installed yesterday and has no conversions yet.
2. **A steady lead generator.** A law firm gets 60 leads a month at about $25 each. It wants
   more leads at the same cost.
3. **A busy store.** An online shop tracks revenue on every order. Its orders range from $15 to
   $400. It makes 200 sales a month at a ROAS of 4.5, and wants at least 4.
4. **A brand campaign.** Pebble wants its ad at the top whenever someone searches "pebble
   coffee".

### Part 2: Budget maths (15 minutes)

Pebble's generic campaign: CPC is about $1.10 and CVR about 5%.

1. What CPA does that imply?
2. Pebble wants 100 conversions a month from this campaign. What monthly and daily budgets
   does it need?
3. What's the most Google could spend on one busy day with that daily budget?
4. The campaign has a $30 daily budget instead. About how many conversions per month does it
   get?
5. Using the break-even CPA of $14.40 from lesson 4, is this campaign profitable on first
   orders?

## Done when

- [ ] All four scenarios have a strategy, any target, and a reason.
- [ ] All five budget questions are answered with working shown.

## Check yourself

1. Scenario answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   1. **Maximize clicks** with a max CPC limit, while you confirm tracking works and conversions
      start arriving. Once tracking is verified, switch to Maximize conversions.
   2. **Maximize conversions with a target CPA of $25**. It has enough volume, and the target
      matches the current reality.
   3. **Maximize conversion value with a target ROAS of 400%**. Order values vary a lot, so
      revenue matters more than conversion count.
   4. **Target impression share**, at the top of the page, with a high share such as 90% and a
      max CPC limit.
   </details>

2. Budget answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   1. CPA = CPC ÷ CVR = $1.10 ÷ 0.05 = **$22**.
   2. Monthly: 100 × $22 = **$2,200**. Daily: $2,200 ÷ 30.4 = **$72.37**.
   3. Up to 2 × $72.37 = **$144.74** on one day. The monthly total still stays within
      $2,200.
   4. $30 × 30.4 = $912 a month. $912 ÷ $22 = about **41 conversions**.
   5. **No.** A $22 CPA is above the $14.40 break-even CPA. The campaign loses money on first
      orders, unless repeat purchases make up for it. Lesson 28 is about fixing that.
   </details>

3. Why not set the first target CPA to $10 when the campaign's real CPA is $22?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Google would bid far lower to try to reach $10, win very few auctions, and the campaign would
   barely spend or learn. Start near $22 and lower it gradually.
   </details>
