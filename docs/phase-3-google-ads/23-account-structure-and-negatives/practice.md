# Practice 23: Structure the account and build negatives

**30 minutes** · **You need:** Your `Keywords` sheet from practice 22 · **Save to:** A new tab named `Structure` in the same sheet. Put a summary in `my-work/notes/lesson-23.md`.

## Goal

Organize your keywords into campaigns and ad groups, and build the negative keyword lists you
launch with.

## Steps

1. **Campaigns:** Decide your campaigns. At minimum, separate brand from non-brand. If your
   practice business has no brand searches yet, plan a brand campaign anyway for later. Name each
   campaign with your naming standard from practice 12.
2. **Ad groups:** Group your kept keywords into 2–4 ad groups per non-brand campaign. Each ad
   group must be one theme you could write a single set of ads for. Use the columns `campaign`,
   `ad_group`, `keyword`, `match_type`.
3. **Ad group negatives:** If two ad groups compete for the same searches, add ad group
   negatives so each search goes to the right one. Note at least one example.
4. **Universal negative list:** Create a list named `Universal negatives` with at least 20
   terms. Use the lesson's categories and your `keep = no` keywords. Choose a negative match
   type for each.
5. **Brand negative list:** Create a list with your brand name and its common misspellings, to
   apply to non-brand campaigns.
6. **Check for over-blocking:** For each negative, ask "Could this block a search I want?"
   Remove or narrow any that could.

## Done when

- [ ] Brand and non-brand campaigns are separate and follow your naming standard.
- [ ] Every kept keyword sits in one themed ad group.
- [ ] The universal negative list has at least 20 terms with match types.
- [ ] The brand negative list exists.
- [ ] You checked the negatives for over-blocking.

## Check yourself

1. When do you create a new campaign, and when a new ad group?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   A new campaign when you need a different budget, bid strategy, location, or other campaign
   setting. A new ad group when keywords need different ads.
   </details>

2. Why separate brand from non-brand campaigns?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Brand searches are cheap and convert very well. Mixed in, they make non-brand performance
   look better than it is, and Smart Bidding may shift spend toward them.
   </details>

3. You add the negative `job`. Does it block "coffee shop jobs"?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   No. Negatives don't match close variants, including plurals. Add `jobs` too.
   </details>

4. You add the negative broad keyword `best`. What could go wrong?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It blocks valuable commercial searches such as "best coffee subscription". Commercial
   modifiers like "best" usually show buying intent.
   </details>
