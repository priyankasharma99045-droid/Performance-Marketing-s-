# Lesson 22: Keyword research and match types

**Phase 3 · Week 5 · Day 2** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

Keywords decide which searches your ads can appear for. Pick the wrong ones and you pay for
people who were never going to buy. Pick the right ones with the right match types and you
reach people at the exact moment they want what you sell.

## Key ideas

### Search intent

Every search has an intent. Match your keywords to the intents that lead to sales:

| Intent | The person wants to | Example | Worth bidding on? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | Learn something | "how to make cold brew" | Rarely for sales. Fine for content. |
| **Navigational** | Reach a specific site | "starbucks login" | Only for your own brand |
| **Commercial** | Compare options | "best coffee subscription" | Yes |
| **Transactional** | Buy or act now | "buy coffee beans online" | Yes, usually your best keywords |

### Where keyword ideas come from

1. **Your customers' words:** From your research in lesson 6.
2. **Google itself:** Autocomplete and "People also ask".
3. **[Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243):** A free tool
   inside Google Ads. Enter seed keywords or a website to get ideas, monthly search ranges,
   competition levels, and estimated top-of-page bids.
4. **Competitors:** The words on their landing pages and ads.
5. **Search terms reports:** Once a campaign runs, what people actually typed. Lesson 27.

Accounts with no spending history see search volume as ranges, such as "1K–10K", not exact
numbers. Ranges are enough to compare keywords.

### Head terms and long-tail keywords

- **Head terms:** Short and broad, such as "coffee". Huge volume, vague intent, expensive.
- **Long-tail keywords:** Longer and specific, such as "organic decaf coffee beans
  subscription". Low volume each, clear intent, often cheaper and higher converting.

Most profitable accounts rely on many mid-length and long-tail keywords.

### Match types

The match type controls how closely a search must match your keyword:

| Match type | Written as | Shows for | Example keyword | Could match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Broad** | `coffee subscription` | Searches related to the keyword's meaning | coffee subscription | "monthly coffee delivery", "tea subscription" |
| **Phrase** | `"coffee subscription"` | Searches that include the keyword's meaning | "coffee subscription" | "best coffee subscription uk", "coffee subscription gift" |
| **Exact** | `[coffee subscription]` | Searches with the same meaning | [coffee subscription] | "coffee subscription", "subscription coffee" |

All three also match **close variants**: misspellings, plurals, and words with the same
meaning.

Broad match reaches the most people and relies on Google's understanding of intent. It works
best with Smart Bidding (lesson 25) and good conversion tracking. Without those, it can waste a
lot of money. Many advertisers start with phrase and exact, then test broad.

### AI Max for Search

**AI Max** is an optional layer on Search campaigns. When it's on, Google can show your ads for
searches beyond your keywords, rewrite ad text from your site, and choose a different landing
page. It can find extra conversions but gives you less control. When you build a campaign,
check whether AI Max is on, and turn it on only as a deliberate choice.

## Common mistakes

- **Bidding on informational searches for a sales campaign:** Clicks, but few buyers.
- **Broad match with no conversion tracking:** Google has nothing to learn from, so it matches
  loosely.
- **Only head terms:** Expensive and vague.
