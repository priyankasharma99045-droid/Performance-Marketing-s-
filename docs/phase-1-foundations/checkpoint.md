## Part 2: Phase quiz (15–20 minutes)

Answer from memory in `my-work/notes/week-02-review.md`, then check.

1. Write the formulas for CTR, CPC, CVR, CPA, and ROAS.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   CTR = clicks ÷ impressions. CPC = spend ÷ clicks. CVR = conversions ÷ clicks. CPA = spend ÷
   conversions. ROAS = revenue ÷ spend.
   </details>

2. A store has a 25% gross margin. What is its break-even ROAS?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   1 ÷ 0.25 = 4.0.
   </details>

3. CPC is $2 and CVR is 4%. What is CPA?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   $2 ÷ 0.04 = $50.
   </details>

4. Name the four funnel stages and one suitable message for each.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Awareness: the problem or benefit. Consideration: proof and how it works. Conversion:
   offer, guarantee, urgency. Loyalty: reorder reminders, referral rewards.
   </details>

5. Why do search ads usually convert better than social ads?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Search captures existing demand: the person is already looking. Social interrupts people
   who weren't looking.
   </details>

6. What's wrong with averaging daily ROAS to get a weekly ROAS?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It gives small and big days equal weight. Total revenue ÷ total spend is the correct
   weekly ROAS.
   </details>

7. Name three parts of an offer.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Any three of: what they get, price, risk reversal, bonus, reason to act now.
   </details>

8. What does "message match" mean?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The landing page continues the exact promise the ad made, so visitors know they're in the
   right place.
   </details>

9. A lead is worth $200 in gross profit if it becomes a customer, and 20% do. What's the
   break-even cost per lead?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   $200 × 0.20 = $40.
   </details>

10. What does ICE stand for, and what is it used for?

    <details><summary>Answer</summary>

    Impact, confidence, ease. You use it to rank test ideas so you work on the most promising
    one first.
    </details>
