# Lesson 9: Landing pages and conversion

**Phase 1 · Week 2 · Day 4** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

The ad gets the click. The landing page gets the sale. If a page converts at 2% and you raise
it to 4%, your CPA halves without changing a single ad. Improving pages is often the cheapest
win in performance marketing.

## Key ideas

### Message match

The page must continue the promise the ad made. If the ad says "20% off your first bag", the
first thing on the page must say "20% off your first bag", not the home page with a menu of
50 products. Every mismatch makes visitors wonder if they're in the right place, and many
leave.

### What a good landing page has

| Element | Job |
|---|---|
| **Headline** | Repeats the ad's promise in plain words |
| **Subheadline** | Adds the main benefit or how it works |
| **Call to action (CTA)** | One clear button: "Get my 20% off" |
| **Visual** | Shows the product or result |
| **Proof** | Reviews, ratings, customer count, press logos |
| **Objection handling** | Guarantee, FAQ, shipping and returns |
| **Trust** | Contact details, secure checkout, real business info |

The most important elements go **above the fold**: the part visible without scrolling. On a
phone, that's very little space.

### One page, one goal

A landing page has one job. Remove menus, extra links, and competing offers. Every extra link
is an exit.

### Form friction

Every form field lowers the share of people who finish. Ask only for what you truly need right
now. Name and email often beat name, email, phone, company, and job title.

### Speed

Slow pages lose visitors before they load. Google measures page experience with **Core Web
Vitals**:

| Metric | Measures | Good |
|---|---|---|
| **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) | How fast the main content appears | 2.5 seconds or less |
| **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) | How fast the page responds to a tap | 200 ms or less |
| **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) | How much the page jumps while loading | 0.1 or less |

Test any page free with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). Always check the
**mobile** result. Most ad clicks come from phones.

### Conversion rate optimization (CRO)

CRO is a repeatable process, not guesswork:

1. **Research:** Where do people drop off? Tools like [Microsoft Clarity](https://clarity.microsoft.com/)
   show heatmaps and session recordings.
2. **Hypothesis:** "If we **[change]**, then **[metric]** will improve, because
   **[reason from research]**."
3. **Prioritize:** Score each idea with **ICE**: impact, confidence, and ease, each from 1 to
   10. Average the three. Start with the highest score.
4. **Test:** Run an A/B test when you have enough traffic. Lesson 34 covers this.
5. **Learn:** Keep the winner, record the result, and start again.

## Example

Hypothesis: "If we change the headline from 'Fresh coffee, delivered' to 'Roasted this week,
at your door in 3 days', then CVR will improve, because reviews show freshness is the top
concern."

| Idea | Impact | Confidence | Ease | ICE score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specific headline about freshness | 7 | 7 | 9 | 7.7 |
| Reduce form from 5 fields to 2 | 8 | 8 | 6 | 7.3 |
| Add video background | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3.3 |

## Common mistakes

- **Sending ads to the home page:** It rarely matches the ad and has too many exits.
- **Testing button colors first:** Small visual changes rarely move results. Test the offer,
  headline, and proof first.
- **Checking only on desktop:** Most visitors use phones.
