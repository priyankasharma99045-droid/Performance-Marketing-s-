# Practice 4: Work out what a customer is worth

**30 minutes** · **You need:** A calculator · **Save to:** `my-work/notes/lesson-04.md`

## Goal

Calculate break-even and target numbers for three situations, and judge whether Pebble's ads
make money.

## Steps

Show your working in your notes for every answer.

### Scenario A: Pebble's targets

Pebble Coffee Co. sells a bag for $36 on average. Each bag costs $21.60 to make, pack, and
ship.

1. Calculate gross profit per order and gross margin.
2. Calculate break-even ROAS and break-even CPA.
3. Pebble wants $4 profit per first order. Calculate target CPA and target ROAS.

### Scenario B: Is Pebble making money?

Use the totals from practice 3: $7,968.84 spend and $20,022.90 revenue across all campaigns.

1. Calculate gross profit on that revenue.
2. Calculate profit after ad spend.
3. Compare the blended ROAS (2.51) with break-even ROAS. What does it tell you?

### Scenario C: The discount

Pebble considers a 20% discount on first orders.

1. What is the AOV after the discount?
2. The cost per bag stays $21.60. What is the new gross profit per order and the new margin?
3. What is the new break-even ROAS?

### Scenario D: Lead generation

Brightsmile Dental earns $600 gross profit from a typical new patient. 25% of leads become
patients.

1. What is one lead worth?
2. A campaign brings leads at $90 each, but only 10% become patients. Is it making money?

## Done when

- [ ] All four scenarios are answered with working shown.
- [ ] You wrote one sentence on what surprised you most.

## Check yourself

1. Scenario A answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   - Gross profit = $36 − $21.60 = $14.40. Margin = $14.40 ÷ $36 = 40%.
   - Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5. Break-even CPA = $36 × 0.40 = $14.40.
   - Target CPA = $14.40 − $4 = $10.40. Target ROAS = $36 ÷ $10.40 = 3.46.
   </details>

2. Scenario B answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   - Gross profit = $20,022.90 × 0.40 = $8,009.16.
   - Profit after ads = $8,009.16 − $7,968.84 = **$40.32**.
   - Blended ROAS of 2.51 is barely above 2.5. Pebble roughly breaks even on first orders.
     The business only makes money if customers come back. That makes LTV, and retention, very
     important for Pebble.
   </details>

3. Scenario C answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   - New AOV = $36 × 0.80 = $28.80.
   - New gross profit = $28.80 − $21.60 = $7.20. New margin = $7.20 ÷ $28.80 = 25%.
   - New break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.25 = **4.0**.

   A 20% discount pushed break-even ROAS from 2.5 to 4.0. The discount has to bring in far
   more customers to pay for itself.
   </details>

4. Scenario D answers?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   - One lead is worth $600 × 0.25 = $150.
   - At a 10% close rate, one lead is worth $600 × 0.10 = $60. Paying $90 per lead loses $30
     on every lead. Cheap-looking leads can still lose money if they don't convert.
   </details>

5. A subscriber is worth $86.40 in gross profit over a year. Pebble pays $20 to acquire one.
   What is the LTV to CAC ratio?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   $86.40 ÷ $20 = 4.3. That passes the common "3 or more" rule of thumb.
   </details>
