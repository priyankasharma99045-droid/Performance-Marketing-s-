# Practice 8: Analyze 10 competitor ads

**30 minutes** · **You need:** A browser · **Save to:** `my-work/notes/lesson-08.md`

## Goal

Find patterns and gaps in your competitors' advertising.

## Steps

1. List 3–5 competitors of your practice business. If you're not sure who they are, search
   your main product on Google and note who advertises at the top.
2. Search each one in the [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) and the
   [Google Ads Transparency Center](https://adstransparency.google.com/).
3. Pick **10 ads** in total. Prefer ones that have run for a long time.
4. Fill in this table in your notes:

   | Competitor | Platform | Angle | Hook | Offer | Format | Call to action | Running since |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

5. Open the landing page for at least 3 of the ads. Note whether the page matches the ad's
   promise. You audit landing pages properly in lesson 9, so save these links.
6. Write 3 patterns you see across the ads.
7. Write 1 gap your practice business could own. Link it to a persona or objection from
   practice 6.
8. Screenshot your 3 favorite ads and save them to `my-work/notes/img/`. This is the start of
   your swipe file.

## Done when

- [ ] 10 ads are analyzed in the table.
- [ ] 3 patterns and 1 gap are written.
- [ ] 3 landing page links are saved for lesson 9.

## Check yourself

1. Why is a long-running ad a useful signal?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Advertisers usually stop ads that lose money. If an ad has run for months, it's probably
   making money. It's a signal, not proof.
   </details>

2. Every competitor leads with "50% off". What are your two options?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Match the expectation with a competitive offer, or stand out with a different angle that
   your research supports, such as quality, speed, or a guarantee. Often you test both.
   </details>

3. What's the difference between learning from a competitor's ad and copying it?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Learning means understanding why it works (the angle, hook, or offer) and applying that
   idea in your own way. Copying reuses their words or design, which makes you look like them
   and can break platform rules and the law.
   </details>
