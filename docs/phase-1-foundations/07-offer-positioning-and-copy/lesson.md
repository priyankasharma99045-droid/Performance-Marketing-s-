# Lesson 7: Offer, positioning, and copy

**Phase 1 · Week 2 · Day 2** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

A strong offer with plain copy beats a weak offer with brilliant copy. Before you write a
single ad, you need to know what you're offering, why it's better, and how to say it in a way
that makes someone stop scrolling.

## Key ideas

### The offer

The offer is the whole deal the customer gets, not only the product:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **What they get** | A 250 g bag of fresh-roasted beans every 2 weeks |
| **Price** | $36 per bag, or about $1.20 a cup |
| **Risk reversal** | Free roast swap if you don't love it. Cancel any time. |
| **Bonus** | Free shipping on subscriptions |
| **Reason to act now** | 20% off the first bag this month |

Risk reversal is often the cheapest and strongest part. It removes the fear of making a
mistake.

### Positioning

Positioning is the place your product holds in the customer's mind compared with the
alternatives. Write it as one sentence:

> For **[target customer]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that **[main benefit]**,
> because **[reason to believe]**.

Example: "For home-office coffee lovers, Pebble is the coffee subscription that tastes like a
cafe, because every bag is roasted the week it ships."

### Features compared with benefits

A **feature** is what the product has. A **benefit** is what the customer gets from it. Use
the "so what?" test:

- "Roasted every Monday." So what?
- "Your beans are never more than 3 days old." So what?
- "Every cup tastes like it came from a cafe." That's the benefit.

Ads lead with benefits and use features as proof.

### Unique selling points (USPs)

A USP is a reason to choose you over the alternatives. Good USPs are:

- **Specific:** "Delivered in 3 days", not "fast delivery".
- **Provable:** You can back it up.
- **Relevant:** They answer a real customer need from your research.

### Three copy formulas

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **PAS** | Problem, agitate, solve | "Supermarket beans taste flat? They sat on a shelf for months. Ours ship 3 days after roasting." |
| **BAB** | Before, after, bridge | "Before: $5 cafe coffee every morning. After: cafe-quality at home for $1.20 a cup. Pebble gets you there." |
| **FAB** | Feature, advantage, benefit | "Roasted weekly, so beans are never stale, so every cup tastes like a cafe's." |

### Headline tactics

- **Be specific:** Numbers beat adjectives. "At your door in 3 days" beats "Fast delivery".
- **Use customer words:** Reuse the phrases from your research.
- **Answer an objection:** "Pause or cancel in one click".
- **Clarity beats cleverness:** People scroll fast. If they need to think to get it, they've
  already gone.

### Length limits shape the copy

| Placement | Limit |
|---|---|
| Google responsive search ad headline | 30 characters |
| Google responsive search ad description | 90 characters |
| Meta primary text (visible before "See more") | About 125 characters |

Practicing short headlines now makes lesson 24 easier.

### Stay honest

Ad platforms reject ads with false or exaggerated claims, and customers stop trusting brands
that overpromise. Only claim what you can prove.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing about yourself:** "We are passionate about coffee" says nothing to the customer.
- **No reason to act:** Without an offer or urgency, people mean to come back and never do.
- **Fake urgency:** A "last chance" offer that never ends damages trust.
