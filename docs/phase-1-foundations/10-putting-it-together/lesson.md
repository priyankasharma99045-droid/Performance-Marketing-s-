# Lesson 10: Putting it together

**Phase 1 · Week 2 · Day 5** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

You now know the funnel, the numbers, the customer, the offer, the competition, and the
landing page. A **marketing brief** ties them into one page that guides every later decision.
Agencies and in-house teams write one before any campaign launches.

## Key ideas

### From business to plan

Every campaign plan answers the same questions, in this order:

1. **What's the goal?** Sales, leads, or sign-ups. Pick one main conversion.
2. **What can the business afford?** Break-even and target CPA or ROAS. Lesson 4.
3. **Who is the audience?** Personas. Lesson 6.
4. **What's the offer, and why this business?** Offer and positioning. Lesson 7.
5. **What does the market look like?** Competitors, patterns, and gaps. Lesson 8.
6. **Where do they land?** The landing page. Lesson 9.
7. **Where do the ads reach them?** Channels for each funnel stage.
8. **How is it measured?** Tracking. Phase 2.

### A first look at channels

You go deep on channels in phases 3 and 4. For now, know what each is good at:

| Channel | Best for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Search** | Capturing people who already want what you sell | They're searching for it |
| **Meta** (Facebook and Instagram) | Creating demand for visual or impulse products | Huge reach, strong automated targeting |
| **YouTube** | Explaining or demonstrating a product | Video holds attention |
| **LinkedIn** | Business-to-business (B2B) | Targeting by job title and company |
| **TikTok** | Reaching younger audiences with native-style video | Entertainment-first feed |
| **Email** | Repeat purchases and nurturing leads | You own the list, and sending is cheap |

### Choosing where to start

A simple rule for a small budget:

- **People already search for the product?** Start with Google Search. It captures existing
  demand.
- **Nobody searches for it yet, but it's visual or quick to demonstrate?** Start with Meta. It creates
  demand.
- **You sell to businesses?** Consider LinkedIn, plus Google Search for your category.

Start with one or two channels and do them well before adding more.

### What makes a good practice business

For the rest of the course, you practice on one business. A good one:

- Has a clear product or service and a clear conversion.
- Has competitors you can research.
- Has some search demand. Check in [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/) or with
  Google autocomplete.
- Is something you understand as a customer.

If your choice from lesson 6 meets these, keep it.

## Example

Pebble Coffee Co. brief, short version:

- **Goal:** Online subscription sign-ups. KPI: CPA.
- **Numbers:** AOV $36, margin 40%, break-even CPA $14.40, target CPA $10.40 for a first
  order. LTV makes a higher CPA acceptable for subscribers.
- **Persona:** Home-office Hannah, who wants cafe-quality coffee without stale beans.
- **Offer:** 20% off first bag, free shipping, cancel any time.
- **Gap:** Freshness and one-click cancellation.
- **Channels:** Google Search to capture "coffee subscription" searches. Meta to create demand
  with video.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with the channel:** "We need TikTok" before knowing the goal, customer, or
  numbers.
- **Too many channels too early:** Each needs budget, creative, and attention.
- **A brief nobody reads:** Keep it to one or two pages.
