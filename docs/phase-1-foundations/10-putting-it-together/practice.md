# Practice 10: Start your marketing brief

**30 minutes** · **You need:** Your notes from lessons 4–9 · **Save to:** `my-work/projects/01-marketing-brief.md`

## Goal

Confirm your practice business and draft the first half of its marketing brief. You finish it
in the phase 1 checkpoint.

## Steps

1. Check your practice business against the four criteria in the lesson. Change it now if it
   doesn't fit. Changing it later means redoing earlier practices.
2. Copy [`templates/marketing-brief.md`](../../../templates/marketing-brief.md) to
   `my-work/projects/01-marketing-brief.md`.
3. Fill in **section 1, The business**.
4. Fill in **section 2, The goal**. Choose one main conversion and one KPI.
5. Fill in **section 3, The numbers**. If the business is real, ask the owner for AOV and
   margin. If not, research typical prices in the category and assume a margin. Mark every
   assumption.
6. Fill in **section 7, Funnel**. Use this lesson's channel table to pick channel ideas for
   each stage.
7. In your notes, write 2–3 sentences: Which one channel would you start with, and why?

## Done when

- [ ] The brief file exists in `my-work/projects/`.
- [ ] Sections 1, 2, 3, and 7 are filled in.
- [ ] Every assumed number is labeled as an assumption.
- [ ] You've written which channel to start with and why.

## Check yourself

1. A local plumber has a small budget. People search "emergency plumber near me" all the
   time. Which channel first, and why?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Google Search. People already search with urgent intent, so search captures ready-to-buy
   demand.
   </details>

2. A startup sells a new kind of reusable notebook that nobody searches for yet. Which channel
   first?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Meta, or another social video channel. Nobody searches for it yet, and it's
   simple to demonstrate visually, so the job is to create demand.
   </details>

3. Why must the brief include break-even numbers before any channel decisions?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Without them you can't tell if any channel is working. Every later decision about budget,
   bids, and what to cut depends on knowing what you can afford per customer.
   </details>
