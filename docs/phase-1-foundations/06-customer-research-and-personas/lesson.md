# Lesson 6: Customer research and personas

**Phase 1 · Week 2 · Day 1** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

Ad platforms increasingly choose who sees your ads by themselves. What you still control is
the message. A message that uses your customers' own words, about their real problem, beats a
clever slogan almost every time. That message comes from research, not guessing.

## Key ideas

### Where to find what customers think

| Source | What you learn | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Reviews** | What people love, hate, and expected | Amazon, Google Maps, Trustpilot, app stores, competitor sites |
| **Forums and communities** | Problems in their own words, and questions they ask | Reddit, Facebook groups, Quora |
| **Search behavior** | What people type, and how often | Google autocomplete, "People also ask", [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/) |
| **Surveys** | Why buyers chose you, what nearly stopped them | A 3-question email to customers |
| **Sales and support** | Common objections and questions | Talk to whoever answers the phone |

Three-star reviews are often the most useful. They're balanced and specific about what fell
short.

### Jobs to be done

People don't buy products. They hire them to get a job done. Describe the job like this:

> When **[situation]**, I want to **[motivation]**, so I can **[outcome]**.

Example: "When I'm rushing to work, I want great coffee at home in two minutes, so I can skip
the expensive cafe queue."

That job tells you the ad needs to talk about speed, quality, and saving money, not about the
origin of the beans.

### What goes in a persona

A persona is a short, research-based profile of one type of customer. Focus on motivation,
not demographics:

- **Who:** A one-line description. "Busy professional, 28–40, works from home 3 days a week."
- **Job to be done:** The situation, motivation, and outcome.
- **Pains:** What frustrates them about the current options.
- **Desired outcome:** What "better" looks like to them.
- **Objections:** What stops them buying. Price? Trust? Effort?
- **Triggers:** What makes them act now. A new job, a bad experience, a holiday?
- **Words they use:** Exact phrases from reviews and forums.
- **Where they spend time:** Which platforms and sites.

### Objections are gold

Every objection becomes an ad, a headline, or a landing page section:

| Objection | Answer it with |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Cost per cup compared with a cafe |
| "What if I don't like the roast?" | Free swap guarantee |
| "Subscriptions are a trap" | "Pause or cancel in one click" |

## Example

Research on coffee subscriptions turns up these real-sounding review phrases:

- "Supermarket beans taste stale."
- "I forget to reorder and run out."
- "I tried a subscription but couldn't cancel easily."

From these you get a persona, **Home-office Hannah**: works from home, used to buy cafe
coffee daily, now wants cafe quality at home. Pains: stale beans, running out. Objection:
subscriptions are hard to cancel. Words she uses: "stale", "run out", "cafe-quality".

Ad idea: "Never run out of fresh beans again. Pause or cancel in one click."

## Common mistakes

- **Making personas up:** A persona from your imagination only repeats your assumptions.
- **Too many personas:** Two or three is plenty. More means none get a focused message.
- **Demographics only:** "Female, 25–34, urban" doesn't tell you what to say in the ad.
