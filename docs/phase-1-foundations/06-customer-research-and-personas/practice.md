# Practice 6: Research customers and write two personas

**30 minutes** · **You need:** A browser · **Save to:** `my-work/notes/lesson-06.md`

## Goal

Collect real customer language and turn it into two personas for a practice business.

## Pick a practice business

You use one business for the rest of the course. Choose it now. You can confirm or change it
in lesson 10.

- **Best:** A real small business you know: a friend's shop, a local service, a family
  business.
- **Also good:** A product category you understand as a customer, such as yoga classes,
  skincare, pet food, or language tutoring.
- **Fallback:** Pebble Coffee Co., the fictional coffee store from the lessons.

Write your choice at the top of your notes.

## Steps

1. Find at least 3 sources of reviews or discussion for your business or its competitors:
   Amazon, Google Maps, Trustpilot, Reddit, or Facebook groups.
2. Collect **20 exact phrases** customers use. Copy them word for word. Include praise,
   complaints, and questions.
3. Type your main product into Google. Note 5 autocomplete suggestions and 3 "People also ask"
   questions.
4. Group your 20 phrases into 3–5 themes, such as "freshness", "price", or "convenience".
5. Write two personas using the structure from the lesson: who, job to be done, pains, desired
   outcome, objections, triggers, words they use, and where they spend time.
6. For each persona, write one ad headline that uses their own words.

## Done when

- [ ] Your notes have 20 exact customer phrases with their sources.
- [ ] The phrases are grouped into themes.
- [ ] Two complete personas are written.
- [ ] Each persona has one headline in customer language.

## Check yourself

1. Why are three-star reviews often more useful than five-star ones?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   They're balanced and specific. People explain exactly what fell short, which shows you the
   objections and gaps your ads need to address.
   </details>

2. Write the jobs-to-be-done sentence structure from memory.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].
   </details>

3. A persona says "Women, 25–34, living in cities". What's missing?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Everything that helps you write an ad: the problem, the motivation, objections, triggers,
   and the words they use.
   </details>

4. A common objection is "I don't trust online reviews of supplements". Suggest one way an ad
   or landing page could answer it.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Many answers work. Examples: show third-party lab test results, offer a money-back
   guarantee, show video reviews from real customers, or show an expert's endorsement.
   </details>
