# Lesson 3: Core metrics

**Phase 1 · Week 1 · Day 3** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

Performance marketing runs on a small set of numbers. Once you know what each one measures
and what moves it, you can read any ad report on any platform.

## Key ideas

### The chain

Every ad result follows the same chain:

```
Impressions → Clicks → Conversions → Revenue
     (people see)  (people visit)  (people act)   (money comes in)
```

Spend sits alongside the whole chain. Each metric describes one link.

### The six core metrics

| Metric | Formula | What it tells you | What moves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CTR** | clicks ÷ impressions | Is the ad interesting to the people who see it? | Ad copy, image, targeting, ad position |
| **CPC** | spend ÷ clicks | How much one visitor costs | Competition, Quality Score, CTR |
| **CPM** | spend ÷ impressions × 1,000 | How much attention costs | Audience size, competition, season |
| **CVR** | conversions ÷ clicks | Does the page turn visitors into buyers? | Landing page, offer, price, traffic quality |
| **CPA** | spend ÷ conversions | How much one customer or lead costs | Everything in the chain |
| **ROAS** | revenue ÷ spend | Revenue for each $1 spent | CPA and order value |

The [metrics cheat sheet](../../reference/metrics-cheatsheet.md) has worked examples for each.

### One formula that explains most problems

```
CPA = CPC ÷ CVR
```

If CPC is $1 and CVR is 5%, then CPA is $1 ÷ 0.05 = $20. There are only two ways to get a
lower CPA: pay less per click, or convert more clicks. When CPA goes up, check which of the two
changed.

### Which metric matters most?

It depends on the business:

- **E-commerce:** ROAS, or CPA when order values are similar.
- **Lead generation:** Cost per lead, and ideally cost per qualified lead.
- **App:** Cost per install, then cost per paying user.

Pick one main metric, called the **KPI** (key performance indicator), and judge campaigns by
it. The other metrics help explain why the KPI moved.

### Benchmarks

You'll find "average CTR" tables online. Treat them with care. CTR depends on industry,
platform, ad format, and audience. A 1% CTR is fine for a Meta ad to strangers and poor for a
search ad on your own brand name. Compare against your own past results and your own targets,
covered in lesson 4.

### Search and social numbers look different

| | Google Search | Meta |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical CTR** | Higher. People are searching for it. | Lower. People are scrolling. |
| **Typical CPC** | Higher | Lower |
| **Typical CPM** | Much higher | Lower |

Don't compare CPM across search and social. Compare what matters: CPA and ROAS.

## Example

Pebble's Google Search campaign last week:

- 10,000 impressions, 400 clicks, $440 spend, 20 purchases, $720 revenue
- CTR = 400 ÷ 10,000 = **4%**
- CPC = $440 ÷ 400 = **$1.10**
- CVR = 20 ÷ 400 = **5%**
- CPA = $440 ÷ 20 = **$22**, which also equals $1.10 ÷ 0.05
- ROAS = $720 ÷ $440 = **1.64**

Is that good? You can't tell without knowing Pebble's margins. That's lesson 4.

## Common mistakes

- **Celebrating a high CTR alone:** Lots of clicks that never buy only cost money.
- **Comparing CPM across platforms:** It measures a different thing on each.
- **Mixing up CVR denominators:** This course uses clicks. Some tools use sessions or
  visitors. Always check.
