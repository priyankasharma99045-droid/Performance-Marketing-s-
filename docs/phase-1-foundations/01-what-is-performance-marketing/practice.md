# Practice 1: Classify real ads

**30 minutes** · **You need:** A browser · **Save to:** `my-work/notes/lesson-01.md`

## Goal

Look at real ads and say what each one is trying to achieve and how its owner could measure
it.

## Steps

1. Copy [`templates/lesson-notes.md`](../../../templates/lesson-notes.md) to
   `my-work/notes/lesson-01.md`.
2. Pick 5 brands you know. Mix big and small, and online stores and service businesses.
3. Open the [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/). Set the country to
   yours and the ad category to **All ads**. Search for each brand and pick 1 ad.
4. Open the [Google Ads Transparency Center](https://adstransparency.google.com/). Search for
   the same brands and pick 1 ad each. If a brand has no Google ads, pick another brand.
5. In your notes, fill in this table for all 10 ads:

   | Brand | Platform | Brand or performance | Goal | Call to action | How they could measure it |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | | | | Awareness, traffic, lead, or sale | The button or phrase | The number they'd track |

6. Under the table, answer in 2–3 sentences: Which ads are capturing demand, and which are
   creating it? How can you tell?

## Done when

- [ ] Your notes have 10 ads in the table.
- [ ] Every ad has a goal and a measurement idea.
- [ ] You wrote the demand capture and demand creation answer.

## Check yourself

Answer in your notes first, then expand each answer.

1. A shoe brand runs a 30-second video with no offer, no website, and a logo at the end. Is
   this brand or performance marketing?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Brand marketing. There's no action to take and nothing to count except views and
   recall.
   </details>

2. Why do search ads usually convert at a higher rate than social ads?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The person searching already wants the product. A social ad interrupts someone who wasn't
   looking.
   </details>

3. Give one example each of paid, owned, and earned media for a coffee shop.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Paid: an Instagram ad. Owned: the shop's website or email newsletter. Earned: a review on
   Google Maps or a mention by a food blogger.
   </details>

4. List the five steps of the performance marketing loop.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Plan, launch, measure, optimize, repeat.
   </details>

5. Pebble pays $9 per 1,000 Instagram views. What is that pricing model called?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   CPM, cost per mille (per 1,000 impressions).
   </details>
