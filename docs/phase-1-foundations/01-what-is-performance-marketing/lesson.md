# Lesson 1: What performance marketing is

**Phase 1 · Week 1 · Day 1** · 25 minutes

## Why this matters

Before you learn any tool, you need a clear picture of the job. Performance marketing is a
loop: spend money on ads, measure what happens, and move money toward what works. Every
lesson in this course teaches one part of that loop.

## Key ideas

### Performance marketing compared with brand marketing

| | Brand marketing | Performance marketing |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Make people remember and like the brand | Get a specific action: a sale, a lead, a sign-up |
| **Measured by** | Awareness surveys, reach, recall | Cost per action and return on ad spend |
| **Time frame** | Months to years | Days to weeks |
| **Example** | A TV ad that ends with a logo | A search ad for "buy running shoes" that leads to a product page |

The two work together. Brand marketing creates demand: people start wanting something.
Performance marketing captures that demand when people are ready to act. A brand nobody has
heard of pays more for every sale.

### Paid, owned, and earned media

- **Paid:** You pay for the space. Google ads, Meta ads, sponsored posts. This course focuses
  here.
- **Owned:** You control the channel. Your website, email list, and social media profiles.
- **Earned:** Others give you attention for free. Reviews, press, word of mouth, shares.

Paid media works best when it sends people to strong owned media, such as a fast, clear
landing page.

### How you pay

Ad platforms run an **auction** every time someone searches or scrolls. Advertisers compete for
the space, and the platform picks which ads to show. You usually pay in one of two ways:

- **Per click (CPC):** You pay when someone clicks. Common on Google Search.
- **Per 1,000 impressions (CPM):** You pay for views, whether or not anyone clicks. Common on
  Meta.

Either way, the result you care about is what happens after: did the person buy or sign up?

### Capturing demand compared with creating demand

- **Search ads capture demand.** Someone types "coffee subscription". They already want it.
  Your ad appears at the moment of need. These ads tend to convert well but the volume is
  limited to how many people search.
- **Social ads create demand.** Someone scrolls Instagram and sees your coffee ad. They
  weren't looking for coffee. Your ad has to interrupt them and make them want it. Volume is
  large, but fewer people act right away.

Most businesses need both. Lesson 37 covers how to choose the mix.

### The performance marketing loop

1. **Plan:** Who to reach, what to offer, how much to spend, and what result counts.
2. **Launch:** Build campaigns and ads.
3. **Measure:** Track what people do after they see the ads.
4. **Optimize:** Cut what doesn't work and put more money into what does.
5. **Repeat.**

### What a performance marketer does day to day

- Checks yesterday's results for anything unusual.
- Reads reports to find what's working and what's wasting money.
- Writes and tests new ads.
- Adjusts budgets and bids.
- Fixes tracking when numbers look wrong.
- Reports results to a manager or client in plain language.

## Example

Pebble Coffee Co., a fictional online coffee store used throughout this course, runs two ads:

- **Ad 1, Google Search:** Shown when someone searches "fresh coffee beans online". Headline:
  "Roasted This Week, at Your Door in 3 Days". Pebble pays $1.10 for each click. Out of 100
  clicks, 5 people buy.
- **Ad 2, Instagram:** A 15-second video of a barista pouring coffee, shown to coffee lovers
  who have never heard of Pebble. Pebble pays $9 for every 1,000 views. Many people watch, a
  few click, and fewer buy that day. Some come back a week later by searching "Pebble
  coffee".

Ad 1 captures demand. Ad 2 creates it. If Pebble judged Ad 2 only on same-day sales, it might
turn it off and later wonder why fewer people search for the brand.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating performance marketing as "cheap clicks":** A click is only a cost. The goal is
  profitable customers.
- **Ignoring brand:** Ads from a brand people recognize get more clicks for the same money.
- **Trusting one number:** Every platform reports its own results generously. You learn why in
  lesson 20.
